What changes at ABRSM Grade 5 aural — and how to master all four parts

Grade 5 is the biggest step-up in the lower-to-intermediate ABRSM aural journey: six-note sight-singing, longer melodies in more keys, texture and musical period questions, and 6/8 compound time. Here is how to train each skill systematically.

By Tim · 15+ years piano teaching · Royal College of Music (MPerf) · Royal Holloway, Univ. of London (PhD)

The four Grade 5 aural tests

5ASing or play back a longer melody from memory

Up to 4 bars in major or minor keys (up to 3 sharps or flats), with wider intervals (4ths and 5ths) and rhythmic detail.

How to practise: stop memorising note-by-note. Hear the opening triad or scale figure as one chunk, and lock onto how the final bar resolves back to the tonic.

5BSight-sing six notes from free-time notation

Up from five notes at Grade 4 to six notes, across keys including C, F, G and D major, reaching up to a 5th from the tonic.

How to practise: always keep the tonic (“home”) ringing silently in your head. Dottino checks every sung note live through your microphone — or lets you read on the piano keys.

5C(i)Features, character, texture & style/period

Alongside dynamics, articulation, tempo and tonality, Grade 5 adds questions on texture (chordal, melody + accompaniment, or contrapuntal two-voice lines), harmonic endings, and style & period (Baroque, Classical, Romantic).

How to practise: always pair a musical label with one audible reason — e.g. “Classical, because of the light Alberti bass and balanced, clear phrases.”

5C(ii)Clap back rhythm & identify 2, 3, 4 or 6/8 time

Compound time (6/8) joins 2, 3 and 4 time, alongside dotted rhythms from real classical excerpts.

How to practise: feel the two swinging dotted-crotchet beats per bar in 6/8 (ONE-2-3 FOUR-5-6) rather than trying to count six fast individual beats.
