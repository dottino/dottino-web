ABRSM Grade 4 sight-singing (Test B): how to stop freezing

Ask any piano teacher which Grade 4 aural test makes students go silent, and almost all of them will say Test B. The good news: it is rarely an ear problem — it is a step-size and confidence problem, and both are very quick to fix.

By Tim · piano teacher for 15+ years, Royal College of Music, PhD in music education · 26 November 2026

What Test B actually asks (and what it doesn’t)

In ABRSM Grade 4 Test B, the examiner places five written notes in front of your child (in treble or bass clef, chosen to suit their voice). The examiner plays the key chord and the starting note, and your child sings the five notes in free time.

  • No rhythm pressure: you can take a calm breath before every note.
  • No “singing exam” voice needed: humming or a soft “la” is completely fine — examiners only listen for pitch accuracy.
  • Small range: the five notes stay inside the first five degrees of the scale (do–re–mi–fa–sol), mostly moving by step.

Why children miss notes in Test B

From fifteen years of teaching and music-cognition research, three habits cause almost all Test B slips:

1Losing the home note (tonic)

Students hear the key chord, then look at the paper and forget how note 1 felt. Once the anchor drifts, every following step lands off-pitch.

2Seeing “up”, singing too big a jump

The eyes spot a higher note and the voice leaps a 4th or 5th when the page only moved by a single step (line to space).

3Fear of hearing their own voice

Many young instrumentalists have never sung solo in front of an adult. In the exam room they whisper or rush all five notes in two seconds.

A calm 4-step routine for home (3 minutes a day)

Step 1 · Hum the anchor: play one note (middle C), hum it gently together until it feels settled.

Step 2 · Point and say “up, down, or same” first: before making any sound, trace the five notes on the page with a finger so the direction is locked in.

Step 3 · Keep steps tiny: think of adjacent line-to-space notes as “next-door neighbours”, not jumps.

Step 4 · Check against the piano afterwards: sing a note, then tap the key to hear whether your voice was slightly high or low — no red marks, just curiosity.
