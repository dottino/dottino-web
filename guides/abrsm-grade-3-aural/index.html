How to prepare for ABRSM Grade 3 aural (Tests 3A–3D)

Grade 3 is where ABRSM aural stops feeling purely intuitive and starts testing four distinct listening skills — including 4 time, pitch-vs-rhythm changes, and major vs minor keys. Here is what each test asks and how to build secure ears in 10 minutes a day.

By Tim · 15+ years piano teaching · Royal College of Music (MPerf) · Royal Holloway, Univ. of London (PhD)

What the four Grade 3 aural tests ask

3AClap the pulse & name 2, 3 or 4 time

The examiner plays a short piece while your child joins in clapping the pulse, stressing the strong beat, and then states whether it is in 2, 3 or 4 time (4 time is new at Grade 3).

Key listening habit: find the bass anchor on beat 1 first, then count calmly until the next strong beat returns — don’t guess on the first bar.

3BSing three short phrases as echoes

The examiner plays three short phrases one by one; your child sings each back immediately in time. (Unlike Grade 4, there is no sight-singing from notation yet.)

Key listening habit: track the overall shape (up, down, arch) rather than trying to memorise isolated note names.

3CSpot a pitch change or a rhythm change

The examiner plays a two-bar phrase twice, changing one spot on the second playing. Your child states whether the change was in pitch or rhythm (and where it happened).

Key listening habit: tap the steady beat silently on the knee on both playings — a rhythm change breaks the grid, while a pitch change moves higher or lower on the same beat.

3DMusical features & major or minor

The examiner plays a short piece and asks about two features: dynamics (gradual or sudden changes), articulation (legato vs staccato), tempo changes, and — brand new at Grade 3 — major or minor tonality.

Key listening habit: listen for the 3rd degree of the tonic chord at phrase endings — bright major 3rd vs darker minor 3rd, regardless of whether the piece is fast or slow.
