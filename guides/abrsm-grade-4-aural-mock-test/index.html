How a Grade 4 aural mock runs in exam order

In a Practical Grade 4 exam, aural takes around 3 to 4 minutes at the end. Many marks are lost simply because the format feels sudden. Running a mock in the exact exam sequence removes that surprise:

1 · Test AEcho a short melody (from memory)

The examiner plays the key chord and starting note, then plays a short melody twice. Your child sings or plays it back keeping a steady pulse.

Mock habit: listen for the shape (up, down, or repeated) on the first hearing, then check the ending note on the second.

2 · Test BSight-sing five notes from the stave

Your child sees five written notes, hears the key chord and the first note, and sings the five notes in their own time.

Mock habit: hold the first note quietly in your head before singing — stepping smoothly beats rushing. Read our full Test B sight-singing guide →

3 · Test C(i)Features & character of a short piece

The examiner plays a short piano piece and asks about one feature (dynamics, articulation, tempo change, or major/minor), followed by the mood or character of the piece and what in the music creates it.

Mock habit: don’t equate speed with major/minor — slow music can still be major, and fast music can be minor. Always listen to the 3rd.

4 · Test C(ii)Clap back the rhythm & say 2, 3 or 4 time

The examiner plays an extract from the same piece twice. Your child claps the rhythm back, then says whether the music is in 2, 3 or 4 time.

Mock habit: feel the heavy beat ONE first, then count lightly until the next heavy beat arrives.

A calm 2-week crash plan (10 minutes a day)

If your exam is under a month away, skip random YouTube playlists and follow this tight 14-day rhythm:

Days 1–2Diagnostic + Test C(ii) pulseTake the 3-minute Ear Check, spot weak areas, lock in 2, 3 and 4 time on real pieces.
Days 3–5Test A melody memoryPractise short 4-to-6-note tunes in major and minor keys; sing or play back steadily.

Quick questions before exam week

Should we still practise aural the night before the exam?

Keep it to one relaxed 5-minute run with easy wins so your child walks in feeling confident, not fatigued.

Does Performance Grade include aural tests?

No — aural and sight-reading are part of ABRSM Practical Grades. Always confirm with your teacher which exam pathway your child is entered for.
